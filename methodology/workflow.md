# AI-PDLC Workflow

## Purpose

AI-PDLC is a risk-driven workflow for developing products where AI agents perform most of the work and humans remain accountable for outcomes.

The workflow exists to control three primary failure modes of AI-assisted development:

- Drift between intent, specification, and implementation.
- Probabilistic errors generated by non-deterministic systems.
- Uncontrolled growth of complexity beyond effective review capacity.

AI-PDLC addresses these risks through versioned artifacts, risk-based autonomy, deterministic verification, human judgment gates, and continuous learning.

This document describes the high-level operational flow of AI-PDLC independent of any specific project, technology stack, or implementation.

---

# Workflow Overview

At its highest level, AI-PDLC operates as a continuous cycle:

```text
Observe
    ↓
Understand
    ↓
Specify
    ↓
Assess Risk
    ↓
Design
    ↓
Plan
    ↓
Implement
    ↓
Verify
    ↓
Review
    ↓
Release
    ↓
Operate
    ↓
Analyze
    ↓
Improve
    ↓
Repeat
```

The cycle is iterative rather than sequential. Any stage may trigger re-evaluation of prior decisions through risk reassessment, specification updates, retrospective findings, or operational feedback.

# Adoption Modes

AI-PDLC supports both greenfield and brownfield environments.

## Greenfield Adoption

For new initiatives, AI-PDLC becomes authoritative from project inception.

All artifacts are created under AI-PDLC governance.

Traceability begins with the first recorded intent.

---

## Brownfield Adoption

For existing projects AI-PDLC begins as an observational companion process.

The methodology first builds an understanding of the current project state:

- existing code
- existing architecture
- existing requirements
- existing delivery process
- existing operational risks

Initially, AI-PDLC mirrors reality rather than governing it.

The process gradually evolves through four stages:

### Stage 0: Observation

AI-PDLC records:

- architecture
- dependencies
- risks
- operational realities

No approvals are required.

No gates are enforced.

The goal is understanding.

### Stage 1: Assisted Production

Pods produce requested artifacts:

- specifications
- risk assessments
- architecture analyses
- retrospectives

Outputs remain advisory.

Human teams continue to own decisions.

### Stage 2: Advisory Governance

AI-PDLC begins evaluating work against its rules.

Examples:

- missing risk classifications
- undocumented scope changes
- insufficient evidence
- incomplete traceability

Warnings are generated but remain non-blocking.

### Stage 3: Controlled Governance

Selected gates become mandatory.

Examples:

- security review
- release review
- risk sign-off

AI-PDLC becomes part of the delivery process.

### Stage 4: Full Governance

AI-PDLC becomes the authoritative workflow.

All work follows risk classification, gate requirements, and traceability rules.

# Core Flow

## 1. Observe

The current state of the environment is gathered.

Inputs may include:

- source repositories
- architecture
- infrastructure
- operational metrics
- incident history
- existing documentation

The purpose is to establish factual context.

No assumptions are treated as truth.

---

## 2. Understand

Relevant context is distilled into durable knowledge.

Outputs may include:

- project inventories
- system maps
- dependency maps
- capability descriptions
- context summaries

Knowledge artifacts are intentionally concise.

AI-PDLC treats context as a limited resource.

---

## 3. Specify

Intent is transformed into a versioned specification.

The specification becomes the authoritative description of expected outcomes.

It includes:

- objectives
- constraints
- assumptions
- acceptance criteria

Acceptance criteria should be represented in a form that can be verified.

---

## 4. Assess Risk

Before execution, work receives a risk classification.

Risk determines:

- agent autonomy
- required evidence
- required reviews
- required approvals

Risk classification is attached to work items rather than projects.

Classifications may change as new information becomes available.

---

## 5. Design

Relevant pods generate proposed solutions.

Examples:

- architecture
- interfaces
- user experience
- data flows
- deployment strategies

Design outputs must remain traceable to the governing specification.

---

## 6. Plan

Work is decomposed into executable units.

Planning prioritizes risk reduction before feature completion.

The objective is early discovery of unknowns rather than maximum parallelization.

Outputs typically include:

- work items
- dependencies
- delivery sequence
- risk mitigation activities

---

## 7. Implement

Implementation pods execute approved plans.

Implementation remains constrained by:

- specification
- architecture
- approved scope
- risk classification

Generated outputs may include:

- code
- content
- documents
- configurations
- infrastructure definitions
- skills
- agent crews

---

## 8. Verify

Deterministic verification occurs automatically.

Examples include:

- compilation
- schema validation
- tests
- contracts
- policy checks
- evaluation runs

Verification determines whether required evidence exists.

Verification does not replace human judgment.

---

## 9. Review

Human reviewers evaluate evidence rather than raw volume of generated work.

Review focuses on:

- alignment with intent
- risk acceptability
- architectural integrity
- operational impact
- specification compliance

Required reviewers depend on risk class.

Approved decisions become versioned records.

---

## 10. Release

Approved work may be deployed.

Release readiness considers:

- risk status
- verification status
- review outcomes
- operational readiness
- rollback capability

Higher-risk work requires stronger release authority.

---

## 11. Operate

Operational pods observe real-world behavior.

They monitor:

- incidents
- regressions
- performance
- security events
- specification drift

Operational reality always takes precedence over planning assumptions.

---

## 12. Analyze

Analysis transforms observations into learning.

Inputs include:

- releases
- incidents
- evaluation results
- review outcomes
- retrospectives

The objective is identifying recurring patterns rather than assigning blame.

---

## 13. Improve

Approved improvements may modify:

- pods
- prompts
- skills
- evaluations
- governance rules
- methodology artifacts

AI-PDLC itself remains subject to continuous improvement.

Methodology evolution follows the same governance principles applied to product work.

---

# Governing Rules

Throughout the workflow the following rules remain invariant:

1. The specification is the source of truth.
2. Risk determines process.
3. Every significant decision is recorded.
4. Every artifact is versioned.
5. Every output must be verifiable.
6. Gates evaluate evidence, not trust.
7. Context is treated as a finite budget.
8. Traceability must remain continuous.
9. Agent capabilities are governed work.
10. AI-PDLC itself is subject to improvement.

---

# Expected Outcome

When applied correctly, AI-PDLC produces a development environment in which:

- agents perform the majority of mechanical work,
- humans remain accountable for outcomes,
- risk controls scale with impact,
- traceability remains intact,
- complexity remains governable,
- learning compounds across projects and time.

The result is not autonomous software delivery.

The result is controlled, evidence-driven product development with AI operating under accountable human governance.
